# Argument prose style guide

How to write reader-facing argument text: grounds, warrant, qualifier, rebuttal,
answer, and node definitions. This file is injected into the AI assistants that
draft these fields and is the checklist for hand-written drafts. Edit it freely;
no code changes needed.

- Write ordinary prose for a general reader: complete sentences, plain words.
- Never coin shorthand (no "the practice-account", "the condition-account").
  Say what the term means instead.
- At most one em dash per field, and only to introduce a definition or
  example. Never use em dashes for parenthetical asides; break the aside into
  its own sentence.
- Grounds: one to three sentences each. State the concrete fact first (who,
  what, when), then why it matters. Assert only what the linked sources show.
- Warrant: one or two sentences that spell out the inference from grounds to
  claim ("So when the national story remembers only one of them, that is a
  choice…").
- Qualifier: state the claim's limits plainly ("This claim asks for equal
  standing, not a swap.").
- Rebuttal: voice the objection fairly, opening with "One could object that…".
- Answer: reply directly, opening with "But…", leading with the strongest
  concrete fact.
- Keep numbers and dates concrete ("one in three adult American men by 1920"),
  never vague ("many", "a large share").
