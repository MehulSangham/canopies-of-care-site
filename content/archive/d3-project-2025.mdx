In 2023 the Heritage Foundation published *Mandate for Leadership: The Conservative Promise*, the central document of what it called Project 2025: roughly nine hundred pages of agency-by-agency policy design, prepared with more than a hundred advisory organisations and distributed free as a PDF. This archive treats it as a primary source, quotable by page, because it is the most explicit statement on record of the displacing tradition translated into governing architecture. Its consistent design principles are the ones this archive has been tracing in opposition: care is conditional, belonging is bounded, and self-reliance is individual.[^1]

The document did not arrive from nowhere. Its funders are substantially the funders of the civic education infrastructure of the previous page: the Bradley, Scaife, Uihlein, Coors, and Koch fortunes, and Barre Seid's trust, whose grants to Project 2025's advisory organisations since 2020 total well over a hundred million dollars, documented in IRS filings and first compiled by investigative reporters in 2024. The same money that teaches the founding-documents-only account of American identity also drafted the plan for installing it.[^2]

## Implementation, By the Author's Own Count

Whether a think-tank blueprint matters is normally a matter of argument. Here it is a matter of record, and the record comes from the plan's own author. In March 2025, an executive order moved disaster preparedness from FEMA toward states and localities, and the agency held roughly ten billion dollars in disaster aid pending immigration-enforcement review; that August, a Homeland Security directive moved to bar federally funded aid groups from serving undocumented residents. In May 2026, Heritage president Kevin Roberts stated on C-SPAN that of 1,913 Project 2025 recommendations, 1,055 had been implemented, and described Heritage's role as "helping to craft public opinion upstream of electoral politics", a first-person confirmation, from the infrastructure's own leader, of how the machinery on the previous page was designed to work. A Heritage fundraising email the following month claimed 53 per cent of the plan was federal policy, a figure that converges with the independent Center for Progressive Reform tracker. When the actor's boast and the critics' count agree, the fact is about as settled as contemporary evidence allows.[^3]

## Displacement in Action

The objection writes itself: Project 2025 is a policy programme, not a cultural project, and policy programmes are ordinary politics. But look at what the policies do to the subject of this archive. When federal disaster relief is restructured to exclude undocumented residents, the disaster mutual aid networks become the only provision available to millions of people, exactly as they were in 1793, 1969, and 1982. And the same legal order that makes them the only option continues to leave them unrecognised by the tax code, unfunded by the programmes, and subject to the food-sharing ordinances that keep returning despite the courts. A policy architecture that produces need, excludes the needy from state care, and burdens the community care that remains is not neutral about which American tradition survives. It is the bounded-care account of the country, encoded in administration.[^4]

This archive has now traced the mutual aid tradition from 1787 to the present, explained the boundedness of its historical forms, and documented the legal, institutional, and discursive machinery arrayed against it. One page remains: what this archive itself is for.
